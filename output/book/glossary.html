Glossary and Concept Index

This glossary is the book’s alphabetical index of technical concepts. Each entry gives a concise reminder and links to the chapter or section that first develops the concept; it does not replace the complete definition, derivation, assumptions, or examples in that source. Mathematical symbols and conventions are recorded separately in the notation registry.

The current entries cover the reviewed material in 1  Linear Algebra Foundations, 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames, 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation, 4  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Implementation, 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow, and 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists. Terminology from a later chapter is added only after that chapter has been completed and reviewed.

A

Active rigid motion
A physical motion that changes a geometric point or rigid body while the reference frame remains fixed. See 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.
Adjoint representation
The coordinate mapping that converts a twist between body and spatial conventions while accounting for both rotation and the change of reference origin. See Spatial twist expressed in the world frame.
Ament build type
The ROS 2 package build integration declared in package.xml; common choices are ament_python for Python packages and ament_cmake for C++ packages. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Angular velocity
The instantaneous rate and axis of rotation of a rigid body relative to a reference frame, expressed in declared coordinates and measured in \(\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\). See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.
Associativity
The property that regrouping operations does not change their result; for matrix multiplication, \((\mathbf A\mathbf B)\mathbf C=\mathbf A(\mathbf B\mathbf C)\). See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations and Planar rotations and SO(2).

B

Basis
A linearly independent set of vectors that spans a vector space, so every vector in that space has a unique coordinate expansion in the basis. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Big-\(O\) notation
A bound describing how rapidly a quantity such as numerical error can scale as an argument approaches a limit. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Body-to-wheel velocity mapping
The inverse differential-drive velocity-kinematics calculation that maps desired body-forward speed and yaw rate to ideal left and right wheel angular speeds. See Relating wheel speeds to body motion.
Body twist
The instantaneous motion of a rigid body relative to the world, with its components expressed along the moving body axes and referenced to the body origin. See Body twist.
Build type
The declared mechanism that tells ROS 2 and colcon how a package is built and installed. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.

C

Child process
A process started by another process; changes made to the child’s environment do not propagate back to its parent shell. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Closure
The group property that combining any two elements using the group operation produces another element of the same set. See Planar rotations and SO(2).
Colcon
A command-line build and test orchestrator that discovers packages in a workspace, orders them by dependencies, and invokes their declared build integrations. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Column space
The set of all linear combinations of a matrix’s columns; equivalently, the set of outputs reachable through multiplication by that matrix. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Commutativity
The property that exchanging operand order does not change the result. Matrix multiplication and rigid-transformation composition are generally not commutative. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations and Why composition in SE(2) is not commutative.
Conjugation
The matrix operation \(\mathbf T\mathbf A\mathbf T^{-1}\), used here to change the coordinate convention of a Lie-algebra element or twist matrix. See Spatial twist expressed in the world frame.
Console script
An executable wrapper generated from a Python package entry point, allowing a named function to be launched as a command or through ros2 run. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Coordinate column
An ordered column of scalar components that represents a geometric object relative to a declared basis or coordinate frame. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Coordinate frame
An origin together with an ordered orthonormal basis used to assign coordinates to points and vectors. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.
Cross product
A three-dimensional vector operation whose output is perpendicular to both inputs, with direction given by the right-hand rule and magnitude equal to the product of their lengths and the sine of their included angle. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.

D

Determinant
A scalar associated with a square matrix whose magnitude gives oriented volume scaling and whose sign records whether orientation is preserved or reversed. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Differential-drive robot
A wheeled robot whose planar forward and yaw motion are produced by independently driven left and right wheels separated by a track width. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Dot product
The Euclidean inner product of two coordinate columns, equal to the product of their lengths and the cosine of their included angle. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Drift
Accumulated estimation error that grows as imperfect incremental measurements are repeatedly integrated without an absolute correction. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.

E

Exact integration
A finite-time state update that evaluates the solution of the stated continuous model exactly under its declared assumptions, rather than approximating the trajectory numerically. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation and 4  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Implementation.
Executable
An installed program that can be launched directly; in a ROS 2 package, ros2 run locates it under the package’s runtime layout. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.

F

Forward differential-drive velocity kinematics
Another name for the wheel-to-body velocity mapping; forward identifies the direction from wheel velocities to body velocity, not the robot’s travel direction. See Relating wheel speeds to body motion.
Forward Euler method
A first-order numerical-integration method that advances the state using the derivative evaluated at the beginning of each time step. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Free vector
A vector with magnitude and direction but no fixed origin, so a coordinate-frame change rotates its components without adding a translation. See Transforming a point by rotation and translation.
Full column rank
The condition \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf A)=n\) for \(\mathbf A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), meaning that all columns are linearly independent and the associated mapping is injective. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.

G

Geometric vector
A basis-independent quantity with magnitude and direction; a coordinate column is its numerical representation in a chosen basis. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Global integration error
The accumulated difference between a numerical trajectory and the exact trajectory over a fixed total interval after multiple integration steps. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Group
A set equipped with an operation satisfying closure, associativity, an identity element, and an inverse for every element. See Planar rotations and SO(2).

H

Handedness
The orientation of an ordered coordinate basis; a right-handed frame follows the right-hand rule, whereas a reflection reverses handedness. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.
Hat operator
A mapping from a twist coordinate column to its structured Lie-algebra matrix representation. See The hat operator and \mathfrak{se}(2).
Homogeneous coordinate
An augmented coordinate column that appends \(1\) to a point or \(0\) to a free vector so rotation and translation can be represented by one matrix multiplication. See Transforming a point by rotation and translation.
Homogeneous transformation
A block matrix combining rotation and translation to map rigid-body point coordinates between frames or to represent an active rigid motion. See 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.

I

Identity element
A group element that leaves every other element unchanged when combined with it; identity matrices are the identity elements of matrix groups. See Planar rotations and SO(2).
Identity matrix
A square matrix with ones on its diagonal and zeros elsewhere that leaves a compatible vector or matrix unchanged under multiplication. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Immutable value
A software value whose stored fields cannot be changed after construction, helping prevent an operation from silently modifying an input state. See 4  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Implementation.
Infinitesimal rigid motion
A first-order direction or instantaneous rate from which nearby finite rigid transformations develop; it is not itself a finite transformation. See The hat operator and \mathfrak{se}(2).
Injective mapping
A one-to-one mapping in which distinct inputs cannot produce the same output; for a matrix map, this is equivalent to a null space containing only the zero vector. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Instantaneous centre of curvature
The planar point about which a rigid body is rotating at one instant and whose instantaneous linear velocity is zero. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Invariant
A property that must remain true for every valid input or operation and can therefore be used as a design check or test oracle. See 4  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Implementation.
Inverse differential-drive velocity kinematics
Another name for the body-to-wheel velocity mapping; inverse identifies the reversed input-output mapping, not backward travel. See Relating wheel speeds to body motion.
Inverse element
A group element that reverses another element so their composition gives the identity. See Planar rotations and SO(2).
Inverse matrix
For a nonsingular square matrix \(\mathbf A\), the matrix \(\mathbf A^{-1}\) satisfying \(\mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf A=\mathbf A\mathbf A^{-1}=\mathbf I\). See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.

K

Kinematics
The mathematical description of motion without modelling the forces or torques that cause it. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.

L

Lie algebra
The linear space of instantaneous directions of a Lie group at its identity, together with a Lie-bracket operation. See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix and The hat operator and \mathfrak{se}(2).
Lie bracket
An operation on Lie-algebra elements; for matrices it is the commutator \([\mathbf A,\mathbf B]=\mathbf A\mathbf B-\mathbf B\mathbf A\). See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix.
Lie group
A group whose elements vary smoothly and whose composition and inverse operations are differentiable. See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix.
Linear combination
A sum of vectors multiplied by scalar coefficients. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Linear independence
The property that a linear combination of vectors equals zero only when every coefficient is zero. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Linear map
A mapping that preserves vector addition and scalar multiplication and can be represented by a matrix after bases are chosen. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Local truncation error
The error introduced by one numerical-integration step when that step begins from the exact state. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.

M

Matrix
A rectangular array of scalars that represents a linear mapping between coordinate spaces once the input and output bases are fixed. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Matrix commutator
The difference \(\mathbf A\mathbf B-\mathbf B\mathbf A\), which measures the failure of two matrix operations to commute. See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix.
Matrix rank
The dimension of a matrix’s column space, equivalently the number of linearly independent columns. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Multilinearity
Linearity of a function separately in each of several arguments; the determinant is linear in each column while the others are held fixed. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.

N

No-sideways-motion constraint
The ideal differential-drive condition that the axle midpoint has zero instantaneous velocity along the body’s lateral axis. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Noncommutative operation
An operation for which changing operand order can change the result; general matrix multiplication and rigid-motion composition have this property. See Why composition in SE(2) is not commutative.
Nonsingular matrix
A square matrix with an inverse, full rank, and a null space containing only the zero vector. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Null space
The set of all input vectors mapped to the zero output by a matrix. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Nullity
The dimension of a matrix’s null space. See Rank-nullity theorem.
Numerical integration
Approximation of a continuous-time state evolution using finite time steps and a finite number of derivative evaluations. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Numerically stable formulation
An algebraically equivalent computational form chosen to avoid avoidable loss of accuracy, undefined intermediate expressions, or fragile special cases. See 4  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Implementation.

O

Orthogonal group
The group \(O(n)\) of real square matrices satisfying \(\mathbf Q^\mathsf T\mathbf Q=\mathbf I_n\). See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Orthogonal matrix
A square matrix whose columns form an orthonormal basis, equivalently \(\mathbf Q^\mathsf T\mathbf Q=\mathbf I\). See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Orthonormal basis
A basis whose vectors have unit length and are mutually perpendicular. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations and 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.
Overlay
A built workspace whose install/ tree is added on top of an existing ROS environment so its packages, Python modules, and executables can be discovered. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.

P

Package manifest
The package.xml file declaring a ROS 2 package’s identity, licence, dependencies, and build type. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Passive coordinate change
A change in the coordinate frame used to describe an unchanged geometric object; nothing physically moves. See 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.
Planar pose
The position and orientation of a planar rigid body relative to a reference frame, commonly represented by two position coordinates and one heading angle. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation and 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.
Planar twist
A three-component representation combining two linear-velocity components and one angular-velocity component for an instantaneous planar rigid-body motion. See The hat operator and \mathfrak{se}(2).
Pose
The position and orientation of a rigid body relative to a chosen reference frame. See 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.
Position coordinate
The coordinates of a geometric point relative to a frame origin, expressed along that frame’s basis axes. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.
Pure rolling
The ideal wheel-ground condition in which wheel rim motion and longitudinal ground displacement agree without slip, giving \(v_i=r\dot\phi_i\). See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.

R

Radian
The dimensionless SI angle measure defined as arc length divided by radius; the label \(\mathrm{rad}\) is retained to communicate angular meaning. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Rank-nullity theorem
For \(\mathbf A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), the identity \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf A)+\operatorname{nullity}(\mathbf A)=n\). See Rank-nullity theorem.
Relative-position vector
A vector directed from one declared point to another, expressed in a declared coordinate frame. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.
Resource index
The installed ament registry that ROS 2 queries to discover package resources and installation prefixes. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Resource marker
A file installed in the ament resource index whose filename registers a ROS 2 package. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Rigid-body point-velocity relation
The equation stating that a body-fixed point’s velocity equals a reference point’s translational velocity plus the rotational contribution from angular velocity and relative position. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames and Velocity of a point fixed to the body.
ROS 2 package
A unit combining source code with the metadata required for building, installing, dependency resolution, and discovery by ROS 2 tools. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
ROS 2 workspace
A directory structure that holds packages developed and built together, conventionally separated into editable source, build state, installed runtime layout, and logs. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Rotation matrix
An orthogonal matrix with determinant \(+1\) that maps vector coordinates between right-handed orthonormal frames while preserving lengths, angles, and orientation. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames.

S

Scalar multiplication
Multiplication of a vector by a real number, scaling its magnitude and reversing its direction when the scalar is negative. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Signed turning radius
The instantaneous distance from the body origin to the centre of curvature with a sign indicating which side of the robot contains that centre. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Singular matrix
A square matrix that has no inverse and maps at least one nonzero input direction to zero. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Sinc function
The unnormalised function \(\operatorname{sinc}(z)=\sin z/z\) for \(z\ne0\), extended continuously by \(\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1\). See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
Skew-symmetric matrix
A square matrix satisfying \(\mathbf A^\mathsf T=-\mathbf A\); planar angular-velocity matrices have this structure. See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix.
Software contract
A precise statement of an operation’s accepted inputs, returned outputs, assumptions, validation, failure behaviour, and observable side effects. See 4  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Implementation.
Source a shell script
Execute a script in the current shell so its environment-variable changes persist after the script finishes. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Span
The set of every linear combination that can be formed from a given collection of vectors. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Spatial twist expressed in the world frame
A twist representation whose velocity field is expressed using fixed world coordinates and referenced to the world origin. See Spatial twist expressed in the world frame.
Special Euclidean group \(SE(2)\)
The group of finite planar rigid transformations that combine a rotation in \(SO(2)\) with a planar translation. See 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.
Special orthogonal group
The group \(SO(n)\) of orthogonal matrices with determinant \(+1\), representing proper rotations in \(n\) dimensions. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations and Planar rotations and SO(2).
Standard basis
The coordinate basis whose \(i\)th vector has entry \(1\) in position \(i\) and zero in every other position. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Symbolic link
A filesystem reference that points to another path; colcon build --symlink-install uses links for supported files so edits to existing source may appear in the installed layout without copying. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.

T

Tangent direction
The derivative of a differentiable curve through a selected point, equivalently the limiting direction of secant lines approaching that point. See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix.
Tangent space
The vector space containing all tangent directions of admissible differentiable curves through a selected point of a smooth set. See Why \dot{\mathbf R}_{wb} is not a rotation matrix and The hat operator and \mathfrak{se}(2).
Track width
The lateral distance between the effective left and right wheel contact lines of a differential-drive robot. See Relating wheel speeds to body motion.
Transformation composition
Combining successive coordinate changes or rigid motions by matrix multiplication; with column vectors, the rightmost transformation acts first. See 6  Planar Rigid-Body Motion: SE(2) and Twists.
Translation
A displacement that changes point positions without changing vector directions or rigid-body orientation. See 2  Geometry and Coordinate Frames and Transforming a point by rotation and translation.
Transpose
The operation that exchanges a matrix’s rows and columns. See 1  Linear Algebra Foundations.
Twist
A coordinate representation of a rigid body’s instantaneous motion combining linear and angular velocity; it is a rate, not a finite pose. See Body twist.

U

Underlay
An existing sourced ROS installation or workspace on which another workspace depends. See 5  ROS 2 Package Development Workflow.
Unit vector
A nonzero vector divided by its Euclidean norm so that its resulting norm is one. See Length and unit direction.

V

Vee operator
The inverse of a declared hat operator, recovering a twist coordinate column from its structured Lie-algebra matrix. See The hat operator and \mathfrak{se}(2).
Velocity kinematics
An algebraic relationship among instantaneous velocities that does not integrate pose or model the forces producing the motion. See Relating wheel speeds to body motion.

W

Wheel-to-body velocity mapping
The differential-drive calculation that maps left and right wheel angular speeds to body-forward speed and yaw rate. See Relating wheel speeds to body motion.
Workspace
See ROS 2 workspace.

Z

Zero-order hold
The assumption that an input remains constant throughout one sampling or integration interval. See 3  Differential-Drive Kinematics and Numerical Integration: Model and Derivation.
